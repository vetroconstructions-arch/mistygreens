2. Power of Attorney (POA) Process: Buying Remotely Without Flying to Pune

Over 80% of our NRI investors complete their property acquisition and registry without physically travelling to India. This is accomplished through a legally executed Special Power of Attorney (Specific POA for Property Purchase):

Step 1

Drafting the Specific POA

The POA document must be customized specifically for the target property (specifying unit/plot number, enclave name, RERA registration, and survey details). Avoid general POAs. The Paranjape NRI Legal Desk provides pre-vetted drafts customized to Maharashtra registration formats.

Step 2

Overseas Execution & Consular Attestation / Apostille

Sign the POA before a public notary in your host nation. Subsequently, have it attested by the Indian Embassy / High Commission / Consulate, or apostilled under the Hague Apostille Convention (accepted in the US, UK, and Europe). Provide two passport-sized photographs and proof of identity.

Step 3

Courier to India & Adjudication at District Registrar

The original attested POA is sent to Pune. Within 90 days of receipt in India, the attorney-holder presents it to the Collector of Stamps / District Registrar for adjudication and payment of stamp duty (nominal ₹500 Maharashtra stamp duty). Once stamped, the POA holder possesses full statutory power to sign the Agreement for Sale, RERA filings, and Sub-Registrar biometric registration.

3. Banking Channels & Payment Flow: NRE vs NRO Accounts

All real estate transactions in India must occur through established banking channels in Indian Rupees (INR). Cash transactions or overseas direct payments to developers are strictly prohibited under FEMA.

  • Non-Resident External (NRE) Account: Funds remitted from abroad in foreign currency are converted to INR. Any property funded directly from an NRE account enjoys automatic repatriation rights — allowing the original purchase principal to be repatriated back to your overseas account without caps.
  • Non-Resident Ordinary (NRO) Account: Used to manage rupee earnings originating within India (rental income, dividends, inheritance). Funds from NRO accounts can also be deployed to purchase property, with repatriation permitted up to USD 1 Million per financial year under RBI's Liberalised Remittance Scheme upon filing Form 15CA/15CB.
  • FCNR (Foreign Currency Non-Resident) Accounts: Fixed deposits maintained in foreign currencies (USD, GBP, EUR) can be liquidated directly towards property installments.

Frequently Asked Questions (NRI Property Investment)

Can an NRI buy NA bungalow plots at Paranjape Forest Trails?

Yes, absolutely. Under Reserve Bank of India (RBI) and FEMA guidelines, NRIs and OCIs are 100% permitted to purchase non-agricultural (NA) residential plots in India without prior RBI approval. The Misty Greens enclave at Forest Trails features PMRDA-sanctioned NA plots with clear marketable titles, making them fully compliant for NRI acquisition.

Do NRIs need to physically travel to Pune to register property?

No. NRIs can execute a Special Power of Attorney (POA) in favour of a trusted family member, representative, or legal advisor. The POA must be signed before a notary or attested by the Indian Consulate/Embassy in your country of residence, sent to India, and adjudicated by the District Registrar in Pune before registration.

Can rental income and sale proceeds be repatriated abroad?

Yes. If the property was purchased using inward remittances via an NRE account, the principal amount invested (equivalent to foreign exchange remitted) can be repatriated freely under the general permission route. Capital gains and rental income deposited in NRO accounts can be repatriated up to USD 1 Million per financial year under RBI guidelines upon submitting Form 15CA and 15CB.
